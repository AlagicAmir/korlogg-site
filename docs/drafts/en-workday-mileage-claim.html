How to claim your mileage in Workday

You drove your own car for work, and your company wants the miles in Workday. Most of the work is not in Workday at all: it is knowing your business miles for the period and having a log to back them up. Here is how the claim usually works, and what to check first.

Before you start

Your employer's setup decides the details. Each organization configures Workday itself, so the names of menus and expense items below can differ from yours. Your company's expense policy and your finance team have the final word.

This is reimbursement, not a tax deduction. Employees cannot deduct their own unreimbursed car expenses on a federal return, so your employer's reimbursement is the way to get this money back.

How the claim usually works

  1. Create an expense report. Open Expenses in Workday and choose Create Expense Report. The mobile app can also be used if your company has it set up.
  2. Add a mileage line. Choose your company's mileage expense item. It may be called Mileage, Personal Car Mileage or something similar. If there are several, for example with different rates, check your policy.
  3. Enter your miles in Quantity. On a mileage line, you normally enter the miles rather than a dollar amount. Workday then applies your company's rate and works out the total.
  4. Attach your mileage log, if your company asks for one. Some employers require a log or a map for each claim.
  5. Check the total and submit. Add any project or cost center your company asks for, then submit the report.

Three things to check

Don't scan the log as a receipt. Receipt scanning looks for an amount, currency, date and merchant. A mileage log has miles, so add it as an attachment on the mileage line instead.

Your company may calculate the distance itself. Some organizations have Workday work out the miles from the addresses you enter. If yours does, your log's miles may differ slightly from the map's. Ask your finance team which one counts.

Your commute may not count. Regular driving between home and your usual workplace is generally personal under IRS rules, and many expense policies leave it out. Claim only what your policy allows.

Which rate applies?

Your employer sets the rate, not you. Many companies use the IRS standard mileage rate, which for business miles in 2026 is 72.5 cents up to June 30 and 76 cents from July 1. As an example, 1,000 business miles at 76 cents comes to $760. If your employer pays above the IRS rate, the part above it is generally treated as wages.

What a useful log contains

For each business trip: the date, where you started, where you went, the business purpose and the miles. Under an accountable plan, you need to show your employer the time, place and business purpose of your expenses, and a log made at the time is much easier to defend than one rebuilt from memory.

Getting the numbers from Körlogg

Körlogg records each drive by itself, and you mark it business or personal and add the purpose. To prepare a claim:

  1. Open Report and tap Export.
  2. Choose the month and the car.
  3. Export the PDF, Excel or CSV file. Export is part of Körlogg Pro.
  4. In the file's summary, 44a Business miles is the number for Quantity.

Before you attach it: the export lists every trip in the period, including personal, medical and charity trips, with their addresses. If you only want your employer to see business trips, export Excel or CSV, keep only the Business rows, and also remove the summary figures you don't want to share.
