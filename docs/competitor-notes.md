# Competitor website notes (2026-10-10)

Ideas only. No text, images or layouts copied; no competitor named on our site; none of their claims reused.

## Observations per site

**driversnote.com (+ .se / .de; /se and /de paths 404)**
- Same template in every language: short hero promise + three benefit lines, one "start free" button, store badges directly under it. Animated phone mockup (trip -> classify -> report) instead of static screenshots.
- Trust = big rating number + review count + three short testimonials; footer shows support phone/email with a live "average response time".
- No FAQ on the homepage; tax detail pushed to guides (SE: milersättning calculator, deductions for enskild firma; DE: 1%-Regel, Pendler- vs Kilometerpauschale, Fahrtenbuch requirements). Demo data on .se/.de still shows miles and USD (weak localisation).
- Hardware beacon gets its own section with price and battery/accuracy benefits.

**mileiq.com**
- Hero + stats bar (users, miles, reviews), then "how it works" as numbered steps with screenshots, toggled by audience (driver/admin).
- Review carousel; repeated "get started" CTA after each section. No FAQ, no tax explanation on the page; education lives in guides and a calculator.

**vimcar.de**
- Badge-heavy (certifications, customer logos, press tests, tax-advisor association). Long homepage ending in a real FAQ.
- FAQ topics: purpose of a Fahrtenbuch, when it beats the 1% rule, what the Finanzamt demands (paper vs digital), audits, mandatory contents, is it required, multiple company cars.
- Three-step "how it works" (get it, set up, export PDF). Visible placeholder text: a reminder to proofread every language.

**everlance.com**
- Hero with rating + app-store badge, audience cards (self-employed / small business / company), six-tile feature grid, closing CTA band.
- Guides that rank: mileage rate of the year, mileage log template, deduction/reimbursement calculators, glossary. Inconsistent user counts across sections.

**triplog.net**
- B2B-first: demo form, three product pillars, stats row. Individuals get a QR code + store badges. No FAQ/tax content on page.

**Mileage Book (mileagebook.com/da, Denmark)**
- Enterprise suite; hero = "software for businesses on the move", demo CTA, customer logo wall, assurance badge.
- Shows company address, phone and registration number in the footer, and a short CEO note: local and transparent.
- Knowledge section: current mileage rates, 60-day rule handbook, webinars. Kørebog rules themselves not explained on the homepage.

**Swedish "körjournal app" results (Boomr, Bokio guide, App Store apps such as Tellit Drive)**
- Results are mostly guides, not app homepages. Boomr's ranking article: förmånsvärde 2026 worked example, what counts as tjänsteresa, manual vs digital table, FAQ (is it required, can employer demand it, savings).
- Bokio guide ranks on: why a körjournal, 10 times / 100 mil rule, 3,000 mil reduction, drivmedelsförmån, trängselskatt, what it must contain, template.
- Small app makers have almost no website; App Store page is their homepage. Gap we can fill.

**German "Fahrtenbuch App" results (sevdesk guide, DriversLog, press tests)**
- Press app tests rank highly and focus on: tamper-evident export, promptness, GPS vs odometer difference, purpose must be entered by a human.
- sevdesk guide: three ways to keep a digital log, paper vs electronic table, what business vs private trips need, archive for 10 years, FAQ.
- DriversLog: old award logos, manuals as PDFs; dated look shows what to avoid.

## What we take and why

1. **Hero:** one-line promise + one sub-line + App Store badge right under it (everyone does this; badge is the only CTA that matters for an iPhone app). Repeat badge after "how it works" and at page end.
2. **"How it works" in 3 steps** with real screenshots: drive (auto log) -> swipe business/private -> export PDF/Excel. Short video loop from the existing screen-recording recipe instead of an animated fake mockup.
3. **True-trust strip** (replaces stars): Made in Sweden by an independent developer · No account needed · Trips stay on your phone (optional own iCloud) · Rules built per country · Support answered personally by the developer · Sources cited with check date.
4. **"What your log contains" table** per country (rows = fields: date, start/end address, odometer, distance, purpose, visited party, driver, reg. number, year-start/end odometer; columns = SE/DK/NO/FI/DE/AT/NL/PL, tick = included in the export). Facts only, linked to the authority's page with "checked YYYY-MM-DD".
5. **Paper vs spreadsheet vs app table** (generic, no brands): time, promptness, completeness, export, what you still must do yourself (purpose).
6. **Country pages** (/se, /de, /dk ...) with local units/currency in screenshots and local terms (körjournal, Fahrtenbuch, kørebog, kjørebok, ajopäiväkirja, rittenregistratie, ewidencja przebiegu).
7. **FAQ section on each country page** (FAQPage schema) - see list below.
8. **Guides (static pages)** - see list below; each ends with an honest "how Körlogg helps" box + badge.
9. **Footer transparency:** developer name, company reg. number, address, support email, "replies personally, usually within a day" (only if true), privacy page.
10. **"Coming in 2.0" note** for iBeacon support (one short block, no date promise).

FAQ topics to add: Is a log required for me? · What must the log contain (per country)? · Can I edit a trip afterwards, and is that shown? · GPS distance vs odometer - which counts? · Where are my trips stored / what if I change phone? · Battery use · Several cars / several drivers · Commuting and private trips - what to record · How long to keep the log.

Guide topics to add: Körjournal: what it must contain (SE) · Förmånsbil: 10 times / 100 mil and the 3,000 mil reduction · Fahrtenbuch vs 1%-Regel (DE) · Mileage allowance rates of the year per country (milersättning, Kilometerpauschale, kørselsgodtgørelse) · Paper vs spreadsheet vs app · Free printable template (PDF) that leads to the app.

## What we deliberately don't take

- Star ratings, review counts, testimonials, user/mile totals (we don't have them; never invent).
- Compliance/approval words: "finanzamtkonform", "Skatteverket-approved", "IRS-proof", "GoBD-compliant", "certified". We state facts the log contains and cite sources.
- Savings promises ("save X kr/€ a year") and average-deduction figures.
- Customer/press logo walls, award badges, demo-booking forms, B2B admin toggles.
- Fake animated mockups with foreign units/currency; pop-up overlays; cookie walls (static site, no tracking cookies needed).
- Any competitor name or comparison against a named product.
