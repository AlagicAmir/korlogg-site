The ATO 12-week logbook

The logbook method lets you claim the work-related share of what your car really costs: fuel, registration, insurance, servicing and its decline in value. The share comes from a logbook kept for at least 12 continuous weeks, and one logbook can serve for five income years. This guide lists what the logbook must contain, journey by journey and for the whole period, and shows the percentage worked out.

Updated 19 November 2026

The three things the method needs

  • A logbook that shows your work-related trips for a continuous period of at least 12 weeks, with odometer records.
  • Receipts or other records of your car expenses.
  • The work-related use percentage from the logbook, applied to those expenses.

What each journey entry must show

Division 28 of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 sets out the entry for a journey, and the ATO's logbook page repeats it:

For each work-related journeyNotes
The day it began and the day it endedUsually the same day
Odometer reading at the start and at the endThe car's own reading, not only the distance
Kilometres travelledEnd reading minus start reading
Why the journey was madeThe ATO asks for the reason and purpose, and the destination

Two rules apply to every entry. It must be made at the end of the journey or as soon as possible afterwards, and it must be in English.

What the logbook period must show

The 12 weeks have their own summary. The law asks for:

  • when the logbook period begins and ends
  • the odometer readings at the start and the end of the period
  • the total kilometres the car travelled in the period
  • the kilometres travelled on work-related journeys
  • the work-related kilometres as a percentage of the total.

The period must be at least 12 continuous weeks and, as the ATO puts it, broadly representative of your travel. Twelve weeks that include your normal run of clients, sites or shifts do that; twelve weeks of holiday do not. If you use the method for two or more cars, keep a logbook for each and make them cover the same period.

Working out the percentage

The ATO's steps are short: total kilometres in the logbook period, work-related kilometres in the period, divide the second by the first and multiply by 100. Then add up the car expenses for the period you are claiming and multiply by the percentage.

Example. Hannah keeps a logbook from Monday 3 August to Sunday 25 October 2026, twelve weeks. The odometer reads 48,210 km at the start and 52,410 km at the end.

StepFigure
Total kilometres in the period52,410 − 48,210 = 4,200 km
Work-related kilometres in the period2,730 km
Work-related use percentage2,730 ÷ 4,200 × 100 = 65%
Car expenses for 2026–27$9,800
Claim9,800 × 65% = $6,370

One logbook, five years

A logbook is valid for five years: the year you keep it and the four that follow. You need a new 12-week logbook earlier if your circumstances change so that it no longer represents your work use. The ATO's examples are changing jobs and moving to a new house.

In each of the four following years you still keep two things:

  • the odometer readings for the start and end of the period you owned the car during the income year
  • your work-related kilometres and use percentage for the year, based on the logbook.

Every income year the law also asks for the car's make, model, registration number and engine capacity. Keep the logbook and the odometer records for five years after the end of the last income year in which you rely on them.

The expense records that go with it

ExpenseRecord
Fuel and oilReceipts, or a reasonable estimate based on the odometer readings for the start and end of the period you owned the car, the car's fuel consumption and the average fuel price in the year
Registration, insurance, servicing, tyres, repairs, lease payments, loan interestReceipts
Electricity for an electric carReceipts from commercial charging stations, and evidence of home charging such as the electricity bill and how you worked out the cost
Decline in valueThe purchase price, and how you worked out the decline in value: the effective life and the method

The purchase price of the car, loan principal repayments, and parking and tolls are not car expenses under this method.

Paper or electronic

The law says what a logbook entry must contain; it does not prescribe paper. The ATO's page names two ways to keep one: an electronic logbook in its own app, or a paper logbook. What matters is that every item in the lists above is there, entered at the time, in English, and that you can produce it for five years.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to log private trips too?

The entries are for work-related journeys. The private kilometres come from the odometer: total kilometres in the period minus the work-related ones.

Can the 12 weeks have a gap?

No. The period must be continuous and at least 12 weeks long.

How long is a logbook valid?

Five years, unless your circumstances change so that it no longer represents your work use. Then you keep a new 12-week logbook.

What if I have no valid logbook?

Then the logbook method isn't open to you for that year. The cents per kilometre method may be: 91 cents for up to 5,000 km per car in 2026–27.
