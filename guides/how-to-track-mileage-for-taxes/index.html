How to track mileage for taxes

If you work for yourself – as a 1099 contractor, a gig driver or a small business owner – every business mile in your own car lowers your taxable income: 72.5 cents a mile in the first half of 2026 and 76 cents from July 1. To claim it you need a log of your trips. This guide shows which miles count, what to record, three ways to keep the log, and how the miles end up on Schedule C.

Which miles count

TripBusiness miles?
From one client, job or delivery to the nextYes
To a client, a supplier, the bank or the post office for the businessYes
From a home that is your principal place of business to another work locationYes
To a temporary work site, when you also have a regular place of workYes
From home to your regular place of work and backNo – commuting
Private errands, also on a work dayNo – personal

The line between business and commuting comes from Publication 463, chapter 4. Our guide on IRS mileage log requirements goes through the exceptions one by one.

What to write down for each trip

  • The date
  • Where you drove – the destination
  • Why – the client, the job or the errand, not just “work”
  • The miles

Once a year you also need the total miles the car was driven, business and personal together. Read the odometer on January 1 and on December 31 and you have it.

The IRS wants the record made at or near the time of the trip. In the words of Publication 463, a log kept weekly “is considered a timely kept record”. So the rule that matters most is simple: do not leave it until the end of the year.

Three ways to keep the log

MethodWhat you doWorks well when
Notebook in the carWrite date, destination, purpose and odometer after each tripYou make a few business trips a month
SpreadsheetFill in the week's trips every Friday; the sheet adds up the milesYou already keep a calendar you can copy from
App with automatic trackingThe phone records every drive; you mark each one business or personalYou drive for work most days

A ready-made spreadsheet is in our mileage log template guide. Whatever you pick, the log has to hold the same details, and Publication 463 says a record prepared on a computer is an adequate record.

A five-minute weekly routine

  1. Every Friday, go through the week's trips and mark each one business or personal.
  2. Add the purpose to every business trip while you still remember it.
  3. Note parking fees and tolls paid on business trips; they are deducted on top of the mileage rate.
  4. On January 1 and December 31, read the odometer.

What your miles are worth in 2026

The IRS standard mileage rate for business is 72.5 cents a mile from January 1 to June 30, 2026, and 76 cents a mile from July 1. Because the rate changed in the middle of the year, add up the miles for each half separately.

An example: 6,000 business miles in the first half and 6,000 in the second.

  • 6,000 × 72.5 cents = 4,350 dollars
  • 6,000 × 76 cents = 4,560 dollars
  • Together: 8,910 dollars off your business income, before parking and tolls

Our IRS mileage deduction calculator does this for your own miles.

One rule to know before your first year: if you want to use the standard mileage rate for a car you own, you must choose it in the first year the car is used in your business. In later years you can switch between the standard rate and actual expenses.

Where the miles go at tax time

  • Schedule C, line 9: your car expenses – business miles at the standard rate, plus business parking fees and tolls.
  • Schedule C, Part IV, line 44: the miles for the year split into business (44a), commuting (44b) and other (44c).
  • Lines 47a and 47b: “Do you have evidence to support your deduction?” and “If ‘Yes,’ is the evidence written?” With a log, both answers are yes.

Common questions

Do I need a mileage log if I use the standard mileage rate?

Yes. The standard rate replaces your gas, insurance and repair receipts, not the log. You still have to show the date, destination, business purpose and miles of each trip and the total miles for the year.

Do gas receipts or bank statements prove my mileage?

No. They show what you spent, not where you drove or why. The IRS asks for a record of each business use of the car. Receipts are useful next to a log, not instead of one.

Is the drive from home to my first job business mileage?

It depends on where your business is based. If your home is your principal place of business, trips from home to another work location in the same business are business miles. If you have a regular place of work outside your home, the drive there is commuting.

Can I deduct mileage if I am an employee?

Not on your federal return, in most cases. For an employee the log is what you hand to your employer to be reimbursed for business miles, and a reimbursement at or below the IRS rate under an accountable plan is not taxed.

How long do I keep the log?

Generally 3 years from the date you file the return that claims the deduction.
