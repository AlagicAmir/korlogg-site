HMRC approved mileage rates 2027/28

Budget 2026 changed the approved mileage rate for the second time in two years. From 6 April 2027 the rate for business miles above 10,000 in a tax year rises from 25p to 35p, while the 55p rate for the first 10,000 miles, introduced in 2026, stays. Here is the full table for 2027 to 2028 next to the current year, how the change was announced, what still has to happen before it is law, and worked examples for employees and the self-employed.

Updated 3 November 2026

The rates from 6 April 2027

The Budget 2026 Overview of Tax Legislation and Rates (paragraph 2.7) says the government will increase Approved Mileage Allowance Payments and the self-employed simplified mileage rates for business mileage above 10,000 miles from 25 pence to 35 pence per mile, with effect from 6 April 2027. The rate for the first 10,000 miles is not mentioned because it does not change: 55p, as in 2026 to 2027.

VehicleFirst 10,000 business milesEach mile over 10,000
Cars and vans, 2027 to 2028 (from 6 April 2027)55p35p
Cars and vans, 2026 to 202755p25p
Cars and vans, 2011 to 2012 up to 2025 to 202645p25p
Motorcycles24p24p
Bicycles20p20p

No change has been announced for motorcycles, bicycles or the 5p passenger payment. The current table on GOV.UK, Travel – mileage and fuel rates and allowances, still ends with the 2026 to 2027 row; the 2027 to 2028 row will appear once the rate is in legislation.

How it was announced, and what is still to come

  • 27 October 2026: the increase was announced the day before the Budget.
  • 28 October 2026: Budget 2026. The Treasury's press release describes it as permanently increasing the generosity of tax-free mileage rates for about 2 million employees and 1 million self-employed people. The policy costings put the cost at £200 million in 2027 to 2028.
  • Later in 2026: the rate will be set by a statutory instrument, ahead of the 2027 to 2028 tax year. Until then the 35p figure is announced policy, not law. The same change applies to the National Insurance disregard for relevant motoring expenditure, so employers will not pay Class 1 NICs on payments up to the new rate either.

The 55p rate for the first 10,000 miles took a different route: it was announced on 21 May 2026, backdated to 6 April 2026 and written into section 230 of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 by section 2 of the Taxation (Energy and Vehicles) Act 2026, which received Royal Assent on 15 July 2026. Section 230 lets the Treasury change the rates by regulations, which is why the 2027 change can be made by statutory instrument.

Worked examples for 2027 to 2028

The approved amount is the most an employer can pay an employee for business miles in their own car without tax or National Insurance, and the amount the employee can claim relief on if the employer pays less. The 10,000-mile threshold is per tax year, counts all of the employee's own vehicles together and starts again on 6 April.

  • 12,000 business miles: 10,000 × 55p + 2,000 × 35p = £5,500 + £700 = £6,200. In 2026 to 2027 the same miles give £6,000; before April 2026, £5,000.
  • 25,000 business miles: 10,000 × 55p + 15,000 × 35p = £5,500 + £5,250 = £10,750, against £9,250 in 2026 to 2027. The further above 10,000 miles you drive, the more the change is worth: £100 for every extra 1,000 miles.
  • 8,000 business miles: 8,000 × 55p = £4,400, exactly as in 2026 to 2027. Below 10,000 miles nothing changes.

The self-employed who use simplified expenses get the same rates: 55p for the first 10,000 business miles of the tax year and, from 6 April 2027, 35p after that, instead of claiming the actual running costs of the vehicle.

If your employer pays less than the approved rate

Employers do not have to pay the approved rate. If yours pays less, you can claim Mileage Allowance Relief on the difference. Example for 2027 to 2028: you drive 15,000 business miles and your employer pays 30p a mile.

  • Approved amount: 10,000 × 55p + 5,000 × 35p = £7,250
  • Paid by your employer: 15,000 × 30p = £4,500
  • Unused balance: £2,750, on which you claim tax relief: £550 at the 20% rate.

HMRC asks for your mileage log with a claim: the date, start and end of each journey and its business reason. What the log needs is in What HMRC needs in a business mileage log; the current-year page, HMRC approved mileage rates 2026/27, has the 55p examples and the backdating to 6 April 2026.

What to do before 6 April 2027

  • Employers: check the mileage rate in your expenses policy and payroll settings. A payment of 35p for miles over 10,000 is tax-free from 6 April 2027; the same payment made on 5 April 2027 is taxable above 25p.
  • Employees and the self-employed: keep the log by tax year. The 10,000 miles reset on 6 April, and a journey's date decides which rate applies.
  • Everyone: watch for the statutory instrument. This page will be updated when it is made and when GOV.UK adds the 2027 to 2028 row.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 35p rate already in force?

No. It was announced at Budget 2026 for 6 April 2027 and will be set by a statutory instrument later in 2026. Until 5 April 2027 the rate above 10,000 miles is 25p.

Does the first-10,000-mile rate change in 2027?

No. It stays at 55p, the rate introduced from 6 April 2026 by the Taxation (Energy and Vehicles) Act 2026. Budget 2026 describes the higher rates as permanent.

Does the change apply to electric cars?

Yes. HMRC's table has one rate for cars and vans, whatever the fuel. For company cars, which earn no approved mileage rate, the advisory electric rate and advisory fuel rates apply instead.

What about National Insurance?

The overview of tax legislation says the change also applies to the National Insurance disregard for relevant motoring expenditure, so the NICs-free amount rises in step with the tax-free amount.
