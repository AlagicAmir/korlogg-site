The 2026 rates

The IRS set the 2026 rates in Notice 2026-10 and raised the business and medical rates from July 1, 2026, citing higher fuel prices. Each trip is valued at the rate in force on the day you drove it.

PurposeJan 1 – Jun 30, 2026Jul 1 – Dec 31, 2026
Business72.5¢ a mile76¢ a mile
Medical (and moving for Armed Forces)20.5¢ a mile23.5¢ a mile
Charity14¢ a mile14¢ a mile

The charity rate is set by law (section 170(i) of the Internal Revenue Code) and does not change with fuel prices.

A worked example

You are self-employed and drive 2,500 business miles in the first half of 2026 and 3,000 in the second half. Your mileage deduction is 2,500 × 72.5¢ + 3,000 × 76¢ = 1,812.50 + 2,280 = 4,092.50 dollars. At a single rate of 72.5¢ for the whole year you would have claimed 3,987.50 dollars, so splitting the year correctly is worth 105 dollars here.

Who can use the standard mileage rate?

Self-employed people, including 1099 contractors and gig drivers, can deduct business miles at the standard rate as a car expense on Schedule C, instead of their actual car costs. The IRS explains the rules, including when you must choose the standard rate, in Publication 463. Business parking fees and tolls can be deducted on top of the rate.

Employees generally cannot deduct unreimbursed car expenses on their federal return. If your employer reimburses mileage, the IRS rate is the usual benchmark: a reimbursement at or below the standard rate under an accountable plan is not taxed as wages. Your mileage log is what supports that reimbursement.

Commuting between your home and your regular place of work is personal and never counts as business mileage.

Medical and charity miles only help if you itemize, and medical expenses only count above 7.5% of your adjusted gross income. The calculator shows their value at the IRS rates so you can add them to your other medical or charitable expenses.

What the IRS expects your mileage log to show

A deduction needs records. For each business trip, the IRS expects the date, the miles, the destination and the business purpose, and you need your total miles for the year to work out the business-use share. Records made at or near the time of the trip are the most reliable. Publication 463 covers recordkeeping in its chapter on records.

Common questions

Why are there two business rates in 2026?

The IRS raised the business rate from 72.5 to 76 cents a mile from July 1, 2026, because of higher fuel prices. Miles driven before July 1 use 72.5 cents and miles from July 1 use 76 cents.

Can I use 76 cents for all my 2026 miles?

No. The 76-cent rate applies to miles driven on or after July 1, 2026. Miles driven earlier in the year stay at 72.5 cents.

Is the mileage deduction the money I get back?

No. It lowers your taxable income. What you save depends on your tax bracket and, for the self-employed, also on self-employment tax.

Can I deduct gas and repairs as well?

Not if you use the standard mileage rate: it already covers gas, repairs, insurance and depreciation. You can add business parking fees and tolls.
