HMRC mileage log template

HMRC does not publish an official mileage log form. GOV.UK says what your records should show, and the layout is up to you. This guide turns those points into a simple template: the columns to keep, a filled-in example, the choice between paper, a spreadsheet and an app, and how long to keep the log.

Updated October 2026

The columns to keep

GOV.UK's page Claim tax relief for your job expenses: vehicles you use for work asks employees to keep records of the dates and mileage of their work journeys. If you send copies of your mileage logs, they must give the reason for every journey and the postcodes for the start and end of every journey. For the self-employed using simplified expenses, GOV.UK says to keep records of your business miles.

That gives four core columns. A few extra columns make the log easier to check and total.

ColumnWhy it is there
DateGOV.UK asks for the dates of work journeys
From (postcode)GOV.UK asks for the start postcode when you send logs
To (postcode)GOV.UK asks for the end postcode when you send logs
ReasonGOV.UK asks for the reason for every journey
Business milesThe number your claim is worked out from
Odometer start and endExtra: ties the miles to what the car actually drove
Business or privateExtra: keeps private trips visible but out of the totals
Vehicle registrationExtra: useful if you drive more than one vehicle

If you claim for more than one job, GOV.UK says to send a mileage log for each employment, so keep one log per job.

A few things to notice:

  • The reason is specific. A client or a job says more than “work”.
  • Each trip starts where the last one ended. The odometer readings run on without gaps, so the totals match the car.
  • The private trip is in the log, with 0 business miles. It is not part of a claim, but it explains the gap in the odometer.

Leave out the drive to your normal workplace. GOV.UK says the relief does not include travelling to and from your work unless it is a temporary place of work, and lists travel between home and work as a cost the self-employed cannot claim. Paper, spreadsheet or app?

GOV.UK lists what to record, not a format. Each option has its strengths.

OptionGood atWatch out for
Paper notebook in the carSimple, always to handTotals by hand, copies to make, easy to fall behind
SpreadsheetTotals and rates add up automatically, easy to copyYou still type in every trip, postcodes and odometer
App that records tripsDate, addresses and distance captured as you driveYou still add the reason and check each trip

Whichever you choose, use the same columns all year. A log that looks the same from April to April is easier to total and to explain.

How often to fill it in

  • After each journey, or at the end of the day. The reason and the postcodes are fresh. Rebuilding weeks of trips from memory is where gaps and guesses creep in.
  • Add up the business miles each month. Small errors are easier to spot in a month than in a year.
  • Start a new total each tax year. The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April. The approved mileage rates for cars and vans from the 2026 to 2027 tax year are 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p after that, so the running total tells you which rate applies.

In the example above, the 30 business miles at 55p come to £16.50. Worked examples are in our guide HMRC approved mileage rates 2026/27.

How long to keep the log

WhoHow long, according to GOV.UK
Self-employedAt least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the tax year
Self Assessment, not self-employed (return sent on time)At least 22 months after the end of the tax year the return is for
Self Assessment, not self-employed (return sent late)At least 15 months after you sent the return

GOV.UK's example for the self-employed: for a 2022 to 2023 return sent online by 31 January 2024, keep the records until at least the end of January 2029. For what each field should contain, see our guide HMRC mileage log: what you need to record.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an official HMRC mileage log template?

No. GOV.UK lists what to record – the dates and mileage, the reason for each journey and the start and end postcodes – but not a particular form, spreadsheet or app.

Which columns should a mileage log have?

Date, start postcode, end postcode, reason and business miles. Odometer readings, a business or private column and the vehicle registration are useful extras.

Should private trips go in the log?

They are not part of a claim. Logging them as private, with 0 business miles, keeps the odometer readings continuous and the totals easy to explain.

How long do I need to keep my mileage log?

If you are self-employed, at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the tax year. Other Self Assessment taxpayers keep records for at least 22 months after the end of the tax year if the return was sent on time.
