Körlogg vs MileIQ vs Everlance

MileIQ and Everlance are the two best-known mileage trackers in the United States. Körlogg does the same job – it logs your drives automatically and gives you a mileage log made to the IRS rules – but with no account, with your trips kept on your own phone, and for a fraction of the price. Here are the differences, with prices from the US App Store.

A third of the price of MileIQ

A year of MileIQ costs $139.99 in the App Store, and a year of Everlance Starter $89.99. A year of Körlogg Pro costs $49.99. If you will be driving for years, unlock Körlogg once for $119.99 and never pay again – less than a single year of MileIQ. Neither MileIQ nor Everlance has a one-time option.

No server – your trips are yours

Körlogg has no server. You don't create an account, and your trips stay on your phone. On iPhone, Körlogg Pro can sync them through your own iCloud – the one your iPhone is already signed in to, your private storage at Apple that only you can open. Lose your phone, break it or get a new one, and your whole log is still there. Körlogg never sees where you drive, and no employer can follow you. Nobody sees anything until you export a report yourself.

With MileIQ and Everlance you sign up, and your drives are kept in their service.

Log as much as you like before you pay

Körlogg logs an unlimited number of drives without paying, with one vehicle and one driver, so you see it working on your own driving before you decide. MileIQ's free plan stops at 40 drives a month and Everlance's at 30 automatic trips – two or three weeks for someone who drives for work every day.

Made to the IRS rules

Körlogg records what the IRS asks a mileage log to show: the date, where you started and finished, the business purpose and the miles of every trip, plus the total miles for the year. Each trip is valued at the IRS standard mileage rate for its own date – 72.5 cents a business mile to June 30, 2026, and 76 cents from July 1 – with medical and charity miles at their own rates and commuting kept apart.

The yearly report, on US Letter as PDF, Excel or CSV, shows the miles by category and the figures for Schedule C Part IV. Every change you make to a trip is listed in the report with the old value, the new value and when it was made.

How to switch

  1. Export your past drives from the app you use today and keep the file.
  2. Download Körlogg and allow location access "Always".
  3. Add your car and its odometer reading. Your next drive is logged by itself.
