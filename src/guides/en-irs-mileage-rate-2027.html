IRS standard mileage rate 2027

The IRS has set the standard mileage rates for 2027. From January 1, 2027, the business rate is ⟦76⟧ cents a mile, ⟦unchanged from the 76 cents that applied from July 1, 2026⟧. The rate for medical and moving miles is ⟦23.5⟧ cents and the charitable rate stays at 14 cents. Here are the 2027 rates next to 2026, which had two rates in one year, how the IRS sets them, worked examples and what to do on January 1.

The notice also sets the depreciation component of the business rate, ⟦35⟧ cents a mile for 2027 (2026: 35 cents), which matters when you sell a car you used the standard mileage rate for. The business rate covers the full cost of running the car: fuel, maintenance, insurance, depreciation. Parking fees and tolls for business trips can be deducted on top.

How the IRS sets the rate, and why 2026 had two

Every December the IRS publishes the next year's rates in a notice, based on a study of the fixed and variable costs of operating a car. The charitable rate is the exception: it is written into section 170(i) of the tax code at 14 cents and does not move. The announcements have come on December 14, 2023, December 19, 2024 and December 29, 2025 for the following years.

2026 was unusual. The year started at 72.5 cents, and in July the IRS raised the business rate to 76 cents and the medical and moving rate to 23.5 cents for the second half of the year, citing increases in the price of fuel (Announcement 2026-11, Internal Revenue Bulletin 2026-29). It had done the same in 2022 and 2011. For 2026 returns that means miles are valued by the half of the year they were driven in; the 2026 mileage deduction calculator does the split for you.

Worked examples for 2027

  • Self-employed, 9,000 business miles: 9,000 × ⟦76⟧¢ = ⟦6,840⟧ dollars deducted on Schedule C. In the first half of 2026 the same miles were worth 6,525 dollars.
  • Gig driver, 25,000 business miles: 25,000 × ⟦76⟧¢ = ⟦19,000⟧ dollars, plus parking and tolls.
  • Employee reimbursed at the IRS rate: an accountable-plan reimbursement of up to ⟦76⟧¢ a mile is not income. Above the rate, the excess is wages. Below it, the difference is not deductible for most employees, since unreimbursed employee expenses are suspended through 2025 and later years under current law.
  • Medical appointments, 600 miles: 600 × ⟦23.5⟧¢ = ⟦141⟧ dollars, deductible only as part of medical expenses above the income threshold.

What to do on January 1

  • Write down the odometer. IRS Publication 463 requires the total miles for the year as well as the business miles; the year-start and year-end readings are how you show both. Take a photo of the dashboard.
  • Use the right rate for each trip. A trip on December 31, 2026 is worth 76 cents; a trip on January 1, 2027 is worth ⟦76⟧ cents. The log must say which day each trip was.
  • Decide the method for a new car. If you want the standard mileage rate for a car, you must choose it in the first year you use that car for business. After that you can switch to actual expenses, but not the other way round for a leased car.
  • Keep the log contemporaneous. Publication 463 says to record each use at or near the time; a weekly log that accounts for the week is timely. What a log must contain is in IRS mileage log requirements.

Common questions

What is the IRS mileage rate for 2027?

⟦76⟧ cents a business mile, ⟦23.5⟧ cents for medical and moving miles and 14 cents for charitable miles, from January 1, 2027.

When did the IRS announce the 2027 rate?

On ⟦Month day, 2026⟧, in ⟦IR-2026-xxx⟧ and Notice ⟦2027-xx⟧. The IRS usually announces the next year's rates in the second half of December.

Does the 2027 rate apply to electric cars?

Yes. The standard mileage rate is the same for every car, van, pickup or panel truck, whatever it runs on.

Can the rate change during 2027?

It can. The IRS raised the rate mid-year in 2011, 2022 and 2026. If it happens again, trips are valued by their date, as in 2026.

Can I deduct my commute?

No. Driving between home and your regular workplace is personal under IRS rules, whatever the rate.
