HMRC mileage log: what you need to record

HMRC has no official mileage log form, but GOV.UK is specific about what your records should show: the date, where each business journey started and ended, why you made it and how many miles you drove. This guide brings HMRC's pages together, with the approved mileage rates that apply from 6 April 2026.

Updated October 2026

Why the log matters

UK mileage claims are worked out per business mile: the approved mileage allowance payment (AMAP) rates for employees, and the flat rates in simplified expenses for the self-employed. Both multiply your business miles, so the miles are the number HMRC may ask you to back up. The log is the record that shows which journeys were business and how far you drove.

What to record for each business journey

GOV.UK's page Claim tax relief for your job expenses: vehicles you use for work tells employees to keep records of the dates and mileage of their work journeys. If you send copies of your mileage logs with a claim, they must give the reason for every journey and the start and end postcodes. Put together, a log covering what GOV.UK asks for contains:

  • the date of the journey,
  • where it started and ended, with postcodes,
  • the reason for the journey (the business purpose),
  • the business miles.

If you claim for more than one job, keep a separate log for each employment. For the self-employed using simplified expenses, GOV.UK simply says to keep records of your business miles; the same four fields are a sensible way to show which miles were business.

The values are made up. A specific reason – a client name or job – says more than “work”, and is what GOV.UK asks for when you send logs with a claim.

What does not count: commuting

Regular travel between home and your permanent workplace is not business mileage. GOV.UK says the relief does not cover travelling to and from your work unless it is a temporary place of work. Our guide Commuting vs business mileage explains the 24-month and 40% rules.

The approved mileage rates from 6 April 2026

According to GOV.UK's travel – mileage and fuel rates and allowances, from the 2026 to 2027 tax year:

VehicleFirst 10,000 business miles in the tax yearEach mile over 10,000
Cars and vans55p25p
Motorcycles24p24p
Bicycles20p20p

For 2025 to 2026 and earlier years the car rate was 45p for the first 10,000 miles. The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April, so the 10,000-mile threshold is counted from 6 April. If your employer pays less than the approved amount, you can claim Mileage Allowance Relief on the difference. Worked examples are in our guide HMRC approved mileage rates 2026/27.

With a company car the approved rates do not apply; you can claim relief on fuel or electricity you paid for on business journeys, and you need records of the actual cost.

How long to keep the log

WhoHow long, according to GOV.UK
Self-employedAt least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the tax year
Self Assessment, not self-employedAt least 22 months after the end of the tax year the return is for
Employers (PAYE records)3 years from the end of the tax year they relate to

Frequently asked questions

Does HMRC have an official mileage log template?

No. GOV.UK lists what to record – dates, mileage, the reason for each journey and the start and end postcodes – not a particular form, spreadsheet or app.

Can I keep my mileage log on my phone?

Yes. GOV.UK asks for the information, not for paper. What matters is that the log holds the details above and that you can send copies when you claim.

What is the HMRC mileage rate for 2026/27?

55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles in a car or van in the tax year from 6 April 2026, and 25p a mile after that.

Do I need to log private journeys?

They are not part of a claim. Logging them as private still helps your totals add up to the miles the car actually drove.
