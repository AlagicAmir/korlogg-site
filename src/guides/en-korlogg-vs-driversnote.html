Körlogg vs Driversnote

Driversnote is the best-known mileage tracker. Körlogg does the same job – it logs your trips automatically and gives you a mileage log built to HMRC's rules – but with no account, with your trips kept on your own phone, and for a third of the price. Here are the differences, with prices from the UK App Store.

A third of the price

A year of Driversnote costs £104.99 in the App Store. A year of Körlogg Pro costs £34.99. If you'll be driving for years, unlock Körlogg once for £119.99 and never pay again – about what a single year of Driversnote costs. Driversnote has no one-time option.

No account, no server

With Körlogg you don't create an account, and your trips stay on your phone. There is no Körlogg server holding your addresses and times. No employer can follow you, and nobody sees anything until you export a report yourself. When you change phones, Körlogg syncs through your own iCloud.

With Driversnote you sign up, and your trips are kept in Driversnote's service.

Log as much as you like before you pay

Körlogg logs an unlimited number of trips without paying, with one vehicle and one driver, so you see it working on your own driving before you decide. Driversnote's free reports stop at 15 trips a month – a week of commuting and client visits.

Built to HMRC's rules

Körlogg records what HMRC asks a mileage log to show: the date, where you started and finished, the business miles and the reason for each journey. Business miles in your own car are valued at HMRC's approved rate for the tax year of the trip – 55p for the first 10,000 miles from 6 April 2026, then 25p – counted across all your own cars and split when a trip crosses the threshold.

Every change is shown with the old value, the new value and when it was made, right in the export, whether you send PDF, Excel or CSV.

How to switch

  1. Export your past trips from Driversnote and keep the file.
  2. Download Körlogg and allow location access "Always".
  3. Add your car's registration and odometer reading. Your next trip is logged by itself.
