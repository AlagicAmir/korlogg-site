How far back can you claim mileage?

If you drive your own car for work and your employer pays less than HMRC's approved rate, or nothing at all, you can claim tax relief on the difference – and not only for this year. GOV.UK allows claims for the current tax year and the four before it. This guide shows which years are still open, the deadline for each, how to claim, the evidence HMRC now asks for, and the separate time limits that apply when you file a Self Assessment return.

Updated 15 November 2026

The short answer: four tax years back

GOV.UK's page on vehicles you use for work says you can claim for the current tax year and the 4 previous tax years. Behind that is section 43 of the Taxes Management Act 1970: no claim for income tax relief may be made more than 4 years after the end of the tax year it relates to. HMRC's guidance on postal claims puts it the same way – your claim must be within 4 years from the end of the tax year you are claiming for.

The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April, so each year's deadline is 5 April four years after it ends. For claims made in late 2026:

Each year is worked out at that year's rate. The 55p rate applies only from 6 April 2026; every earlier year uses 45p for the first 10,000 business miles. Current rates and the 10,000-mile rule are explained in HMRC approved mileage rates 2026/27.

What you can claim for each year

Mileage Allowance Relief is on the gap between the approved amount and what your employer paid, for business journeys in your own vehicle. Ordinary commuting does not count – see Commuting vs business mileage. Worked example for 2022 to 2023, the oldest year still open:

  • 7,000 business miles, employer paid 25p a mile: £1,750
  • Approved amount: 7,000 × 45p = £3,150
  • Unused balance: £1,400; relief at the 20% basic rate is worth £280

The relief is tax back, not the balance in cash, and GOV.UK says it cannot be more than the tax you paid in that year. You must also have paid tax in the year you claim for.

How to claim

  • Online or by post (form P87), if you do not send a Self Assessment return. HMRC's postal guidance limits postal claims to total expenses of £2,500 or less per tax year and accepts them only on the current P87 form.
  • Self Assessment, if your expenses for the year are more than £2,500, or if you already send a return – GOV.UK says you must then claim through the return instead.

For the current tax year HMRC usually adjusts your tax code so you pay less tax. For previous years it either adjusts your tax code or pays a refund.

The evidence HMRC asks for

Since 14 October 2024 HMRC requires supporting evidence before it processes a PAYE claim for employment expenses, because of what it calls a growing risk from ineligible claims. For mileage that means:

  • a copy of your mileage log for each employment, showing the reason for every journey and the postcodes where it started and ended;
  • whether your employer reimbursed any of the cost and, if so, evidence of how much – payslips or the expense claims your employer paid are the obvious documents.

That is the hard part of a late claim. A log written at the time is the strongest evidence; for earlier years you may have to build one from what you still have: your work diary, emails and timesheets, the expense claims you submitted, and the addresses of the clients or sites you visited. GOV.UK does not set a separate standard for logs put together later, so the log still needs every journey's reason and start and end postcodes. GOV.UK's guidance for the self-employed on lost records says that where you cannot give actual figures you should do your best, and tell HMRC which figures are estimates. What a complete log contains is in What HMRC needs in a business mileage log.

If you send a Self Assessment return

Here the time limits are different. You can correct a return within 12 months of the Self Assessment deadline, online or by sending another paper return: GOV.UK's example is that a 2024 to 2025 return usually has to be changed by 31 January 2027. That applies to employees claiming relief in their return and to the self-employed claiming business mileage.

Once that window has passed, the route is a claim for overpayment relief, which can be made up to 4 years after the end of the tax year it relates to. It must be made in writing and include that you are claiming overpayment relief, the tax year, why you think you paid too much, how much, and whether you have appealed the same payment, with a signed declaration; GOV.UK warns that a claim missing any of this will be rejected. Schedule 1AB of the Taxes Management Act 1970 lists cases where HMRC need not give the relief – for example where you could have claimed it by amending your return in time and knew, or should reasonably have known, about it.

For the self-employed there is one more limit. If you use the simplified mileage rate for a vehicle, HMRC's manual (BIM75005) says the method must be kept for as long as the vehicle stays in the business, and you can only change to or from actual costs when the vehicle is replaced. A vehicle on which capital allowances have been claimed cannot use the mileage rate. A late claim cannot switch a car's method for earlier years.

Keep the records afterwards

If you are self-employed, GOV.UK says to keep records for at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the tax year. For other Self Assessment returns sent on time, at least 22 months after the end of the tax year. Keep the mileage log with the claim, so you can answer questions about it later.

Frequently asked questions

How many years can I backdate a mileage claim?

The current tax year and the 4 before it. In late 2026 that is back to 2022 to 2023, whose claims must reach HMRC by 5 April 2027.

Can I claim for 2021 to 2022?

No. The 4-year limit for that tax year ended on 5 April 2026.

Can I use 55p for old years?

No. 55p applies to business miles from 6 April 2026. Earlier years are worked out at 45p for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p after.

Do I need a separate claim for each year?

Each tax year has its own approved amount, rate and deadline, and HMRC's £2,500 limit for postal claims is per tax year. Work out and evidence every year on its own.

What if I no longer have a mileage log for an old year?

HMRC asks for a log with the reason and start and end postcodes of every journey. Rebuild it from records made at the time, such as your diary, emails and the expense claims your employer paid, and do not guess journeys you cannot support.
