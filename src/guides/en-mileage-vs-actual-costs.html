Mileage vs actual costs for the self-employed

If you're a sole trader and drive for your business, you can claim your car in one of two ways: a flat rate per business mile (simplified expenses), or the business share of what the car actually costs to run plus capital allowances on buying it. The choice is made per vehicle and, once you've used the flat rate for a car, you're bound to it for as long as that car is in the business. This guide explains who can use which method, compares them at a few mileages and lists the records each one needs.

Updated 15 November 2026

The two methods

  • Simplified expenses (flat rate): you multiply your business miles by HMRC's rate. For 2026 to 2027 that is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles of cars and goods vehicles and 25p after; motorcycles are 24p. Budget 2026 announced that the rate above 10,000 miles rises to 35p from 6 April 2027 (see HMRC mileage rates 2027/28). The rate replaces the actual costs of buying and running the vehicle, such as insurance, repairs, servicing and fuel.
  • Actual costs: you add up the running costs – insurance, repairs and servicing, fuel, hire charges, vehicle tax, breakdown cover – and claim the business share. The cost of buying the car is not an expense; you claim it through capital allowances, again only for the business share.

Both methods cover only business driving. Travel between home and your place of work, and other non-business driving, can't be claimed under either; Commuting vs business mileage explains where HMRC draws that line.

Simplified expenses are optional and work with both the cash basis and traditional accounting. You can use the flat rate for your vehicle and still work out every other expense at actual cost.

You can't switch for the same vehicle

GOV.UK puts it plainly: once you use the flat rates for a vehicle, you must continue to do so as long as you use that vehicle for your business. The law says the same in section 94D of the Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005: if the mileage deduction is made, no other deduction is allowed for that vehicle's costs in that or any later period. It works the other way too. If you've ever claimed capital allowances on a vehicle, you can't move it onto the flat rate, because, as HMRC's manual BIM75005 explains, the rate already contains an allowance for depreciation.

The choice comes round again when you replace the vehicle: the new one can go on either method. So the decision to get right is the one you make in the first year you use a car for the business.

Worked comparisons for 2026 to 2027

A sole trader buys a second-hand petrol car for £12,000. Its CO2 emissions are over 50g/km, so it gets capital allowances at the special rate of 6% a year. Running costs are about £1,300 a year (insurance, vehicle tax, breakdown cover, servicing) plus about 20p a mile for fuel, tyres and repairs. The car is also driven 4,000 private miles a year. The figures are made up; your own receipts decide the result. The business share is business miles divided by total miles; the percentages are rounded, the amounts are not.

The flat rate for 20,000 miles is 10,000 × 55p + 10,000 × 25p. From 6 April 2027 the same miles would give 10,000 × 55p + 10,000 × 35p = £9,000. With the 55p rate, the flat rate comes out ahead for a modest car at every mileage here.

Actual costs tend to win when the car is expensive to buy or to run. The clearest case is a new, unused electric car bought before April 2027: it qualifies for a 100% first-year allowance. Bought for £36,000 and used 10,000 of 14,000 miles for business, it gives a first-year allowance of about £25,714 (10,000 ÷ 14,000 of £36,000), plus the business share of its running costs. The relief comes almost all in the first year, though: later years get only running costs, and when you sell the car the sale price comes back as a balancing charge, again for the business share. Compare over the years you expect to keep the car, not just the first.

How capital allowances work for a sole trader's car

  • Rates: new, unused cars with zero CO2 emissions get a 100% first-year allowance if bought before April 2027. Other cars with emissions of 50g/km or less, and second-hand electric cars, go in the main rate: 14% a year from April 2026 (18% before). Budget 2026 announced a further cut to 12% from 6 April 2027 for Income Tax. Cars over 50g/km go in the special rate, 6% a year.
  • Private use: a car you also drive privately goes in its own single asset pool, and you reduce the allowances you claim by the private use proportion. The annual investment allowance doesn't cover cars.
  • Cash basis: even under the cash basis, the cost of buying a car is claimed through capital allowances, not as an expense.
  • Leased cars: you claim the hire charges as a running cost; for a car leased from 6 April 2021 with emissions over 50g/km, 15% of the hire charge is disallowed.

BIM75005 expects a contemporaneous record of business mileage to support any flat-rate claim; under actual costs, the same log is what proves the business share. What a good log contains is in What HMRC needs in a business mileage log. Keep the records for at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the tax year.

Frequently asked questions

Can I change from the flat rate to actual costs next year?

Not for the same vehicle. Once you've used the flat rate for it, you must keep using it for as long as the vehicle is used in your business. You can choose again for the next vehicle.

Can I claim parking on top of the flat rate?

Yes. Parking, tolls and congestion charges on business journeys are not included in the mileage rate. Other travel, such as train fares, can also be claimed on top.

I have a limited company. Can it use the flat rate?

No. Simplified expenses can't be used by limited companies or partnerships with a company as a partner. If you're a director driving your own car, the company can pay you HMRC's approved mileage rate instead.

Does the 10,000-mile threshold reset every year?

Yes. It runs with the tax year, from 6 April to 5 April. If your accounting period doesn't match the tax year, BIM75005 shows how to apply each year's rates.
