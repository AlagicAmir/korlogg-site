Mileage tracker apps 2026 – an honest comparison

Most "best mileage tracker app" lists are written by someone who sells one of the apps. So is this one: we make Körlogg. That is why every fact about the other apps comes from the maker's own website, help pages or App Store page, with the date we checked it, and why we say plainly when another app suits you better. The page covers the UK and the US; prices are given in pounds and dollars.

Prices, plans and ratings checked 15 November 2026

The quick answer

  • You drive on your own, in the UK or the US, and don't want an account: Körlogg. Trips stay on your phone, logging has no monthly trip limit without paying, and Pro can be bought once instead of every year.
  • You want receipts, expenses and mileage in one app: Everlance or TripLog. Both log expenses with receipt photos, and both can pull in transactions from a linked card or bank. Körlogg has no expense tracking.
  • You are self-employed in the US and want help with the tax side too: Everlance Professional includes 1099 tax filing, and Stride is free and finds deductions from your mileage and expenses.
  • A manager or admin should collect and approve your reports: Driversnote Teams, MileIQ for Teams or TripLog's business plans. They have web tools for that; Körlogg has no admin view.
  • You want to work on your trips on a computer: MileIQ, Driversnote or TripLog Premium, which all have a web dashboard. Körlogg runs only on the phone and iPad.
  • You only drive a few business trips a month and want a free report: MileIQ's free plan (40 drives a month) or Driversnote's (reports for 15 trips a month). Körlogg logs without limit for free, but the export needs Pro.

Körlogg

Körlogg notices when you start driving, records the trip with GPS and ends it when the car has stood still. You swipe a trip business or private and add the reason. With a UK car, business miles in your own car are valued at HMRC's approved rates for the tax year from 6 April; with a US car, each trip is valued at the IRS rate for its date and can be marked commute, medical or charity. Changes made afterwards are listed in the export.

Strengths: no account and no Körlogg server, so nobody sees your trips until you export them. Logging has no trip limit without paying. Pro can be bought once instead of as a subscription, and the yearly price is lower than the others' paid plans. Pro adds several cars and drivers and odometer reconciliation, and on iPhone iCloud sync in your own Apple account and an iBeacon per car.

Weaknesses: Körlogg is new and has few ratings. Without Pro there is no export, so the free version gives you the log in the app but not the report. There is no web dashboard, no admin view for teams, no expense or receipt tracking and no link to accounting software.

MileIQ

MileIQ has the most App Store ratings in the US of the apps here. It detects drives in the background, and you classify them in the app or on the web dashboard. Reports come as PDF and a spreadsheet; in the UK the 10,000-mile split shows in the XLS version.

Strengths: long track record, a web dashboard, several vehicles with Bluetooth assignment, and a Teams product. The free plan covers 40 drives a month.

Weaknesses: you need a login, and unlimited drives cost £94.99 or $139.99 a year. On 15 November 2026 MileIQ's UK help page still gave 45p as the 2026 example rate, so check the rate in your settings against GOV.UK.

Driversnote

Driversnote has the most App Store ratings in the UK of the apps here, and many in the US too. It starts tracking at a set speed or with its own iBeacon in the car, and you can review trips on the web. Reports are PDF or Excel, with HMRC or IRS rates or your own.

Strengths: a web version, a separate log per vehicle, Teams with admin approval and SSO, an edit log and an API on Pro, and trip status in CarPlay and Android Auto.

Weaknesses: without paying, reports cover 15 trips a month. Pro costs £104.99 or $150 a year in the App Store (the website shows £8 or $11 a month before tax). You need an account, and trips are kept in Driversnote's service.

Everlance

Everlance combines mileage with expenses and, in the US, tax help. Trips are recorded in the background, and you can limit recording to work hours. In the UK its listing names the 55p and 25p rates.

Strengths: receipts and expenses in the same app, automatic expense capture from a linked card on Professional, and in the US a deduction finder, 1099 tax filing and audit defense on Professional. Exports in PDF, Excel and CSV.

Weaknesses: the free plan has 30 automatic trips a month. In the UK it has few ratings so far (55). On 15 November 2026 Everlance's pricing page showed two sets of US prices ($89.99 and $119.99 a year, and $69.99 and $99.99 a year); we list the App Store prices.

TripLog

TripLog tracks automatically without a monthly trip limit and offers several ways to start a trip: phone GPS, the car's Bluetooth, a TripLog Beacon, or a TripLog Drive box that records without the phone. It also tracks income, fuel and other expenses.

Strengths: a lower yearly price than MileIQ, Driversnote and Everlance (£50.99 or $59.99), receipt scanning, bank and card feeds, QuickBooks Online and SAP Concur on Premium, and business plans with approvals.

Weaknesses: on the free plan you get reports only with the one 7-day pass a year, so in practice the report needs Premium. Premium is paid yearly; we found no monthly price. You need an account.

Stride

Stride is a free app for independent workers in the US, from a company that also helps people enroll in health insurance. It tracks mileage, expenses and income and creates IRS-ready reports you can give an accountant or upload to TurboTax; it does not file your taxes.

Suits: US gig and freelance workers who want a free app that also finds deductions. Suits less well: anyone who wants trips recorded without starting them, because Stride's help centre says you start each drive yourself (the iPhone app can remind you when you are moving and the phone is charging), and anyone in the UK, where it is not in the App Store.

How to choose

  1. Who needs to see the trips? Only you: an app without an account is enough. A manager or accountant on an ongoing basis: choose an app with a web dashboard or a team plan.
  2. How many trips do you drive? Above 15 trips a month (Driversnote), 30 automatic trips (Everlance) or 40 drives (MileIQ), the free plan no longer covers everything.
  3. Mileage only, or expenses too? If receipts and bank feeds matter, Everlance or TripLog do more than Körlogg.
  4. Subscription or one-time purchase? Over several years a one-time purchase costs less than a subscription. Körlogg's one-time £119.99 or $119.99 costs about as much as one year of MileIQ or Driversnote, or less.
  5. What does the tax authority need? Neither HMRC nor the IRS prescribes an app; what counts is what the log contains. Read what HMRC needs in a mileage log or the IRS mileage log requirements, and work out your figure with the IRS mileage deduction calculator.
