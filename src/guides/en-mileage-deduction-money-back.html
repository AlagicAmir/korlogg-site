Mileage deduction 2026: how much money do you get back?

Every business mile you drive in your own car is worth money. If you are self-employed, each mile takes 72.5 or 76 cents off the income you are taxed on, which for most people means 17 to 26 cents less tax per mile. If you are an employee, your employer can pay the same miles back tax-free. Drive 10,000 business miles a year and that is roughly 1,700 to 2,600 dollars that stays with you. Here is how the money adds up, and what you need to claim it.

Self-employed: where the money comes from

The IRS standard mileage rate for business is 72.5 cents a mile from January 1 to June 30, 2026 and 76 cents from July 1. Your business miles times the rate is a business expense on Schedule C, line 9. It lowers your profit, and a lower profit lowers two taxes at once:

  • Self-employment tax. The rate is 15.3%, charged on 92.35% of your net earnings. Every dollar of mileage deduction saves about 14 cents here, whatever your income tax bracket.
  • Income tax. The same dollar also leaves your taxable income, saving 12, 22 or more cents depending on your bracket.

That is why the mileage deduction is worth more to a self-employed driver than almost any other single expense: one log, two taxes. Business parking fees and tolls are deducted on top of the rate.

Employees: get the miles paid back

If you drive your own car for your employer, you can't deduct the miles on your federal return, but your employer can reimburse them. Paid under an accountable plan, a reimbursement up to the IRS rate is not taxed and does not show up as wages: at 76 cents a mile, 400 business miles in a month are 304 dollars back in your pocket.

What you need is the same log: the date, where you drove, why and how many miles, handed in within a reasonable time; Publication 463 treats 60 days after the trip as reasonable. Ask your employer what rate they pay, and send the log every month.

Medical and charity miles

  • Medical: driving to the doctor, hospital or pharmacy counts as a medical expense at 20.5 cents a mile to June 30, 2026, and 23.5 cents from July 1. Medical expenses are deductible to the extent they are more than 7.5% of your adjusted gross income, if you itemize.
  • Charity: miles driven as a volunteer for a qualified charity are worth 14 cents each, if you itemize.

The miles people leave on the table

  • From one client, job or delivery to the next
  • To the bank, the post office or a supplier for the business
  • To a temporary work site
  • From a home office that is your principal place of business to a client
  • To a business meeting, a training course or a trade show

A single forgotten 30-mile round trip is about 22 dollars of deduction. One a week is more than 1,100 dollars a year. The drive between home and your regular place of work is the one that does not count: that is commuting.

Missed last year?

You can generally amend a return within 3 years after the date you filed it, or within 2 years after the date you paid the tax, whichever is later. If you drove for business and never claimed it, our guide IRS mileage log requirements shows what your records must contain before you do.

What you need to claim it

A log. For every business trip: the date, the destination, the business purpose and the miles, written at or near the time, plus the total miles the car was driven in the year. The IRS says plainly that estimated amounts are not deductible, so the log is what turns your driving into money.

Common questions

How much is 1 mile worth on my taxes?

For a self-employed person in 2026, one business mile is a deduction of 72.5 cents to June 30 and 76 cents from July 1. In federal tax actually saved, that is roughly 17 to 26 cents a mile, depending on your income tax bracket.

Do I get the mileage deduction back as a refund?

The deduction lowers the income you pay tax on. If you have already paid in more than you owe, through estimated payments or withholding, the difference comes back as a refund; otherwise it lowers what you still have to pay.

Is it better to deduct mileage or actual car expenses?

The standard mileage rate needs no gas or repair receipts and is usually simpler. Actual expenses can be higher for an expensive car driven mostly for business. If you want the standard rate for a car you own, you must choose it in the first year the car is used in your business.

Can I claim mileage without receipts?

With the standard mileage rate you don't need gas receipts. You do need the mileage log, and receipts for parking fees and tolls if you deduct them.

Can an employee deduct unreimbursed mileage?

Generally not on a federal return. The way to get the money is a reimbursement from your employer, which is tax-free up to the IRS rate under an accountable plan.
